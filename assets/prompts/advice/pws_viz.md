description: Problems Worth Solving for data visualization


1. **Buried finding:** A plot contains many curves or panels, but the central comparison or result is difficult to identify.

2. **Inaccurate visual encoding:** Pie slices, bubble areas, or 3D shapes make small numerical differences difficult to judge.

3. **Incorrect data mapping:** Plots use wrong columns, mismatched observations, incorrect aggregations, or fabricated values despite rendering successfully.

4. **Distorted scales:** Truncated bar axes exaggerate differences; unlabeled logarithmic scales or inconsistent panel scales mislead comparisons.

5. **Concealed distributions:** Means and error bars hide outliers, skewness, multimodality, or important differences between individual observations.

6. **Incorrect uncertainty:** Error bars confuse standard deviations, standard errors, and confidence intervals, or ignore pairing between observations.

7. **Misleading aggregation:** Pooled results conceal subgroup reversals, failed experiments, or substantial differences across experimental conditions.

8. **Unexplained graphical elements:** Acronyms, symbols, colors, lines, or shaded regions lack clear labels or nearby explanations.

9. **Overlapping elements:** Legends obscure data, annotations collide with curves, or tick labels overlap and become unreadable.

10. **Broken publication layout:** Figures contain clipped labels, illegibly small text, pixelated graphics, or missing elements at final publication size.
