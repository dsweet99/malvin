description: Investigate causes, acquire knowledge systematically

Briefly state the problem you are trying to solve.

Repeat until you think you've solved the problem (or until you've invested more time that you think is appropriate):
 - Hypothesize: Hypothesize one falsifiable explanation of the cause of the problem.
 - Predict: Define a falsifying test. If the hypothesis were true, what outcome would the test produce?
 - Falsify: Run the test. If falsified, reject the hypothesis.



## Logging

Log your hypotheses and test results -- as they become available -- to {{ exp_log }}.
When you are all done, append a brief executive summary and a super-brief tl;dr to the log, and echo both to the
chat/context directly.

## Evidence
- Evidence stands on its own, independent of you.
- Evidence is ideally reproducible. A reader should understand from your writing how to reproduce the evidence,
  or, at least, how to find out how to reproduce it.
- Evidence is ideally based on direct observation -- measurements of some kind. Reference to a prior
  publication of good evidence is acceptable, too (although you should state how confident you are
  in the publication's evidence)
- Keep an evidence log in {{ exp_log }} for reference by you and by future agents.

## Generalization

Prefer a "weak hypothesis", a rule that solves the observed problem without unnecessary conditions. Remove a condition only if the rule still distinguishes success from failure.

“Weak” means less restrictive, not vague, uncertain, or merely short. Weak hypotheses generalize better. Contrast
this with Occam's razor, which suggests that *simpler* hypotheses generalize better. (Weaker and simpler may
be correlated, but we think weakness is the right thing to focus on.)
