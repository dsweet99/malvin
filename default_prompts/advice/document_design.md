Beautiful document design follows the foundational **C.R.A.P.** principles—**Contrast, Repetition, Alignment, and Proximity**—to make pages readable, organized, and attractive.

## Core Design Rules

- **Contrast:** Make different elements look distinct by varying font sizes, weights, colors, or spacing so key headings and focal points stand out immediately.
- **Repetition:** Consistency builds a professional theme; repeat visual elements like color schemes, heading styles, bullet formats, and margins throughout the document.
- **Alignment:** Every item on the page should line up with another element to create a clean, intentional structure that guides the reader's eye smoothly.
- **Proximity:** Group related items—such as a caption next to a photo or a heading above a paragraph—close together while separating unrelated sections with ample white space.

## Typography and Hierarchy Rules

- **Limit Font Sizes:** Use no more than 2 to 3 typeface sizes (such as a main title, a heading, and body text) to establish a clear visual hierarchy.
- **Breathing Room:** Leave generous negative space around blocks of text and images so the layout does not feel cluttered or overwhelming.
- **Intentional Visuals:** Use high-resolution photography or structured tables sparingly to break up long text and clarify data relationships rather than just for random decoration.
