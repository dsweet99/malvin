# Hypothesize, test, generalize

Use this guidance selectively, not as a checklist. Match effort to the uncertainty and consequences. Routine, reversible work usually needs only a direct check.

## Core loop

1. **Identify the uncertainty that matters.** What do you need to know to choose your next action? When the cause is unclear, consider plausible competing explanations. Treat your explanations as conjectures, not evidence.

2. **Choose a discriminating test (Popper).** State what your hypothesis predicts and what result would contradict it. Choose the smallest reliable test that could change your next action. Seek a meaningful counterexample rather than easy confirmation.

3. **Evaluate the evidence and act.** Reject or revise hypotheses contradicted by trustworthy evidence. Check whether an apparent contradiction instead reflects noise, faulty measurement, or a failed assumption. Surviving a test supports provisional use; it does not prove truth.

## Evidence quality is essential

Prefer direct measurements, reproducible results, inspectable logs, and primary sources with clear methods. Check that evidence actually addresses the claim. Your confidence, another LLM’s agreement, and repeated copies of one claim are not independent evidence.

Use additional methods only to resolve a specific doubt: for example, a baseline comparison, negative control, held-out case, or replication. Never invent results or quietly change success criteria.

## Generalize without unnecessary restrictions

Following Michael Timothy Bennett’s idea, prefer a rule that solves the observed problem without unnecessary conditions. Remove a condition only if the rule still distinguishes success from failure. Check a relevant new case when practical.

“Weak” means less restrictive, not vague, uncertain, or merely short. Bennett’s formal results depend on assumptions; this advice is a heuristic.

## Stop appropriately

Stop when evidence sufficiently supports the requested action and remaining uncertainty is unlikely to change it. Report material limitations. Say “I don’t know” when evidence is inadequate.
