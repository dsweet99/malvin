description: Problems Worth Solving for writing

- **Mystifying synonymy**: Confusing the reading by referring to the same thing by two different terms.
    Example: "Dog is man's best friend. You should take good care of your canine."  Just say "dog" twice.
- **Non-local reference**: An offset (e.g., parenthetical) definition that isn't right next to the noun it defines.
    Example: "A pound of prevention (an imperial measure of weight) is worth a pound of cure." should be "A pound (an imperial measure of weight) of prevention is worth a pound of cure."
- **Misinterpretable "this"** (or "that", "these", "those") are uses of these pronouns where it may be unclear what the antecedent is. It's better to reference it explicitly.
    Example: "blah blah pretzels. These are making me thirsty." should be "...blah blah pretzels. These pretzels are making me thirsty."
- **Unnecessary intro**: It is not necessary to say "X matters because" or "X is important because". We can just tell the reader about the importance directly.
- **Unsubstantiated throw-away**: An extra phrase that sounds like "there's more to the story", that you're referring to something well-known that the reader should know, but there's no support in the text through reference or data.
    Example: "and related settings".  You should either say what the related settings are or not mention them.
- **Lack of specificity**: It's better to use a specific/precise term than to use a generic one.
    Example: "The test failed because of a problem with the environment." would be better as "The test failed because MALVIN_CODEX was not set."
- **Negative parallelisms**: Contrast framing ("It's not X, it's Y") corrects a claim nobody made, which adds false drama. It's better to just state Y then discuss it.
    Example: "The optimization didn't improve because of good luck. It improved because we used RAASP sampling." would be better as "The optimization improved because we used RAASP sampling."
- **Reflexive rule of three**: The rule of three is a good one, but don't use it if there aren't really three things to communicate.
    Example: "The new runner is faster, safer, and more reliable" Better: "The new runner finishes in 40 seconds instead of 90."
- **Inflated style vocabulary**: These are fancy verbs and adjectives that add tone but no content. (The word "delves" comes up a lot.)
    Example: "This study delves into the intricate interplay between caching and latency, underscoring its crucial role." Better: "This study measures how caching changes latency."
